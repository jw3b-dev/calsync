calsync

Privacy policy

Last updated 29 October 2026.

Who runs it

calsync is run by John Wellard, for himself. It is used with two accounts only, both his own: a Google account and an Outlook.com account. It has no other users.

What it reads

The events in John's main Google calendar and in his Outlook.com calendar, from 30 days back to a year ahead: their title, time, place, description and free/busy status, and who is invited (only to tell whether an event is a meeting, which calsync never changes).

What it keeps

On John's own home server, readable only by calsync:

  • the sign-in tokens for the two accounts;
  • a small database that pairs each event with its copy: the two event IDs, a fingerprint of the content, the start and end times, and whether the event repeats;
  • a log of each run: how many events it copied, changed or removed, and their event IDs.

It does not keep event titles, descriptions, places or the names of people invited. Those pass through memory while a copy is made and are not written anywhere except the other calendar.

Where it sends it

Event details go from one of John's calendars to the other, between Google and Microsoft, and nowhere else. calsync has no analytics, no advertising and no third-party services, and it does not use calendar data to train AI models.

calsync's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Stopping it

Removing calsync's access at myaccount.google.com/permissions stops it at once. Its tokens and database can then be deleted from the server.

Changes and questions

Any change to this policy is published on this page with a new date. Questions: open an issue on this site's GitHub repository.
